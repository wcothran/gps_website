The comparison contract

One metricEvery panel reflects the same selected result.
One levelChoose LEA or School and keep the entity selector aligned.
Up to five peersUse a small, relevant comparison set.
One populationKeep time period and student group consistent.

Seven metric categories

  • Academic Achievement
  • College & Career Readiness
  • Graduation Rate
  • High School Student Success
  • Multilingual Learners Progress
  • Prep for Success
  • School Environment
Match the controls. School comparison level needs schools selected; LEA comparison level needs LEAs. Optional sliders can silently exclude an entity.

Build the question

  1. Select category and metric.
  2. Select comparison level.
  3. Choose up to five entities.
  4. Add optional value, percentile, or denominator ranges only if needed.

Read the panels in order

  1. Selected Data Summary: current, prior, change, percentile, denominator.
  2. Trend over time: sustained direction and divergence.
  3. Change from previous year: most recent movement.
  4. Overall distribution: position in the broader field.
  5. Student groups: where a broad gap becomes specific.
Denominator is part of the result. Use it to judge scale, volatility, suppression, and whether percentages are reasonably comparable.

Interpretation checks

  • Do not call a one-year movement a trend.
  • Distinguish percent change from percentage-point change.
  • Check denominator changes behind sudden movement.
  • Do not treat percentile as a complete performance judgment.
  • Use student-group differences as follow-up questions, not causal explanations.
Small groups can move sharply. Review denominators and availability before sharing subgroup comparisons.

Strong follow-up

Find a peer with a sustained pattern, verify the denominator and subgroup profile, then investigate practices and context outside the dashboard.
